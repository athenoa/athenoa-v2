Living it

A down-to-earth guide to Athena’s teaching

Athena teaches a great deal of knowledge, and keeps saying it is not enough: it has to become how you live. This page takes that at its word, and turns the teaching into things you can do on an ordinary Tuesday.

A working draft, not for publication. Everything here comes from the 173 practices and 90 sets of stages already gathered in Nostos from 27 of Athena’s talks, and each step links to the talk it comes from. The seven phases, the order, the times of day, the minutes and the sample day are an arrangement made for this draft, not Athena’s own, and Athena has not seen any of it.

The big words, on a Tuesday

Most of Athena’s key words are older than our ideas of them. Here is what each one asks of you in practice.

  • παιδεία

    paideia

    Not schooling. Schooling fills you with information; paideia turns the eye of the soul from outside to inside, and changes the way you see. “Know thyself” is its shortest road.

    On a TuesdayThe moment you notice you are reacting, and look at the reaction instead of at whatever set it off. ↗

  • γνῶθι σαυτόν

    know thyself

    Two stages. First, know your preferences, personality, body, desires, feelings and thoughts. Then know that which knows them: the observer itself. You are not what you have.

    On a TuesdayWhen you are irritated, asking not only “why am I annoyed?” but “who is watching the annoyance?” ↗

  • εὐδαιμονία

    eudaimonia

    Not luck and not a good mood. Luck turns on fortune’s wheel; eudaimonia is a stable fullness that holds whatever happens. Athena notes it is what we now call awakening or enlightenment.

    On a TuesdayThe car horn still makes you jump. You just don’t spend the next hour being thrown about by it. ↗

  • κάθαρσις

    catharsis

    Cleaning, and mostly by taking away. Truth is reached by subtraction: the views, habits and passions that make you suffer are washed out, and what was always there shows through.

    On a TuesdaySaying no to the third glass, the late-night scrolling or the old argument, and noticing how much lighter it feels. ↗

  • ἦθος · ἀρετή

    ethos · virtue

    Ethics, Athena says, does not mean morality: it means habits of energy. Virtue is the easy flow of the soul, and non-virtue is whatever blocks that flow.

    On a TuesdayTreating a sulk not as a sin but as a blockage, and asking what would let it move again. ↗

  • ἔρως

    eros

    The force that moves the whole path. Not attachment and not control: to love means “I am the ally of your freedom”. It widens from one person to a city to the whole.

    On a TuesdayBeing glad, after the first pang, when someone you love takes the chance that will take them away from you three days a week. ↗

  • λόγος

    logos

    Not thinking. Logos is the one in you that observes what you live, the energy of consciousness itself.

    On a TuesdayThe part of you that can see you are about to send that email in anger, while you are still angry. ↗

  • ἀπορία

    aporia

    Not knowing, on purpose. Starting from “I know that I do not know” opens the pores; renouncing knowledge is the beginning of knowledge.

    On a TuesdayGoing into a conversation with a real question instead of a position. ↗

  • ἐξαίφνης

    suddenly

    Awakening cannot be scheduled. You ripen, and it comes in its own hour, often through a shock or a great love that shakes a boxed-in life awake.

    On a TuesdayKeeping up the practice without checking whether it is “working”. ↗

  • ἐμμένεια

    immanence

    Remaining in. First the One is sought beyond everything; then it is recognised in everything, the tax office included. The journey ends with a return, not a fairy tale.

    On a TuesdaySilently greeting “my dear One” in the person who has just pushed in front of you. ↗

Seven phases of the way up, and back

Athena describes the ascent in many ladders: the Cave, the second birth of the soul, Apollo’s five names, the ladder of virtues, the stages of Pythagorean paideia. They don’t line up one to one, but they tell the same story, and here they are laid side by side as seven phases.

Two of Athena’s warnings belong at the start. The phases are not a timetable: the decisive step comes “suddenly”, and nobody can schedule it. And they are not a ranking: as long as someone is checking which step they are on, that someone is still the one who has not arrived. Read them as a map of where you might be, never as a scoreboard.

One road, many maps

Athena holds that truth is one: those who awaken in any tradition live the same state, in the colour of their own culture and place, and Athena asks us to look in our own root first. So each phase is set beside the other roads, with the Greek one as the trunk. Rest on any of them (or tap it) to see the link. ↗

1

φαίνεται

Phase 1: The itch

Something in you says: this can’t be all.

What it feels like

  • You are fed up, and the thought “this can’t be all” keeps coming back.
  • A loss, an illness, a break-up or a great love has knocked something loose.
  • You catch yourself searching: books, podcasts, videos at one in the morning.
  • The adolescent questions are back: who am I, why am I here, what is all this for?

What shifts

The search stops being a mood and becomes a decision.

What to do

  1. Read the pain as an alarm clock

    When pain, loss or grief arrives, pause before you fix anything, and ask what condition it is calling you to change.

    Pain in becoming is the soul’s wake-up call out of sleep. ↗

  2. Take the exhaustion seriously

    Treat being fed up as the push to start looking for what is true, not as a mood to cure with a holiday.

    Pain is the perfect motive to lead us to truth. ↗

  3. Keep the adolescent questions

    Write them on the first page of a notebook (who am I, why am I here, what is existence for?) and don’t rush to answer them.

    These questions start the path that leads to self-knowledge. ↗

  4. Admit that you don’t know

    Say it honestly, and let it make you ask, as the restless prisoner asks whether things are really as he was told.

    Asking opens the pores, and Apollo begins to reveal himself. ↗

  5. Leave the door open

    Truly want change, and leave a little room for eros.

    Even the universe respects free will: wanting is what turns the wheels. ↗

Watch out for

  • Curing the itch with a purchase, a trip or a new relationship, and calling it solved.
  • Banging on the cave wall: looking for the way out in your circumstances. Banging only takes you deeper; the way out is inward.

2

μεταστροφή

Phase 2: The turn

You decide to look inward, and you choose a way to do it.

What it feels like

  • You have said, out loud or on paper: “I want to know myself.”
  • You have found a teaching that speaks to you, and perhaps a few people who want the same thing.
  • Everything is new and slightly dizzying: what you took for real looks less certain.

What shifts

The teaching stops being interesting information and becomes something you do.

What to do

  1. Decide to know yourself

    Say “I want to know myself”, and give your inner world the attention you would give a loved one’s heart.

    The desire to know is the first step of knowledge. ↗

  2. Write your intention

    Ask why you are here and what you truly want from this. Write it in the notebook, date it, and plan to re-read it in a year.

    To see whether your motive has been transformed, fulfilled, or has vanished. ↗

  3. Start from not knowing

    Begin with “I know that I do not know”, and empty out what school, the news and habit taught you as truth.

    Truth, like death, is subtractive. ↗

  4. Dig deep in one place

    Choose one path and one teaching, and commit to study and practice over time.

    You will not find the alchemist’s gold by digging one-metre holes here and there, as social media tempts us to. ↗

  5. Look in your own root first

    Before running East, study the tradition you started from, and look for the practices hidden in its texts.

    Everything you seek is where you started. ↗

  6. Choose helpers well

    Look for a teacher or guide whose motive is pure and who has walked the path. Then still do the inner work yourself.

    No one can lead you on a road they have not crossed, and no one can walk it for you. ↗

  7. One minute of gratitude

    Before each study session, pause for a minute and recognise three things: your ripened wish to be free, your access to a teaching, and the companions who share it.

    Tradition calls this meeting a rare and blessed conjuncture; gratitude is the key that opens the path. ↗

Watch out for

  • Spiritual shopping: a retreat here, a course there, never staying long enough for anything to grow.
  • Turning the new truth into a banner and preaching it at dinner.

3

κάθαρσις

Phase 3: The cleaning

The long middle: watching yourself, and washing out what makes you suffer.

What it feels like

  • You start to notice your thoughts, moods and reflexes as they happen, and it is not always flattering.
  • Old habits and passions come up exactly when you are trying hardest. Athena says this is how it goes: they surface to be cleansed.
  • Some days it is “extremely therapeutic”. Some days it feels like going to war.

What shifts

Watching yourself stops being an effort and becomes the background of the day. Virtue starts to feel less like discipline and more like ease.

What to do

  1. Observe yourself through the day

    Ask: what am I desiring, what am I feeling, what quality did that emotion just have? Catch the daydream: where did this thought start, and where is it going?

    This stage restores the soul’s free flow. ↗

  2. Before, during, after

    Before an act, look at your motive and your energy. During it, keep watching them. Afterwards, review them.

    A Pythagorean practice that purifies the passions and steers action to the good. ↗

  3. Say «No», then plant a «Yes»

    Name one old state that tosses you about and refuse it. Then ask what you will build in its place, and tend it: water, prune, be patient.

    A No not accompanied by a Yes is lame, and stays locked to what it opposes. ↗

  4. Replant a habit

    With a clear intention, stop expressing one trait that does not help you, and sow a good habit where it was.

    You move from passive observer to active maker of your character. ↗

  5. Dry the passions

    Notice fear, anger, jealousy and envy as wet, sticky states. Turn inward, and cultivate the virtue on the other side of each.

    Heraclitus: the dry soul is wisest and best. ↗

  6. Purify the senses

    With each contact ask: what am I receiving, what satisfies me, what do I lose? Move towards what benefits and away from what harms.

    The senses are a path of purification, not something to escape. ↗

  7. Purity before power

    Lay a base of virtue before you chase energy, intensity or influence, and then put that force at the service of wisdom, never of passing passions.

    Without purity the vital fire becomes Plato’s black horse. ↗

Watch out for

  • Self-flagellation. Respect your shame instead: “I’m human, I erred, I deceived myself here. What can I learn?” Then adjust and move on.
  • Rushing to theory. Athena’s ladder of virtues puts purification before theory, because knowledge without virtue is dangerous.

4

κυοφορία

Phase 4: The long night

Something new is growing in you, and it needs protecting.

What it feels like

  • You are unsure what is real, and people close to you may think you have gone a little mad.
  • Signs and coincidences: a book falls open at the right page, a stranger says the very thing you needed.
  • Rock-bottom moments. In the Cave, the darkest night comes nearest the exit.

What shifts

Not something you do. The birth comes “suddenly”, when it is ripe.

What to do

  1. Protect what is growing

    Stay quiet, humble and close to yourself. Take only pure “food”, on every level. Avoid quarrels and shadow-boxing with people still bound, and don’t be discouraged if you are called mad.

    The embryo of awakening is vulnerable and must be hidden from what mocks it. ↗

  2. Find the “I am”

    Close eyes, ears and mouth to the outside, and attend to what stays stable while everything changes: the same “I am” at five, at twenty and at a hundred. Stay there.

    This is the apprenticeship that carries the second birth to term. ↗

  3. Go down with the hero’s torch

    In the dark season, look for your monsters and passions. Trace their roots in events, family and ancestors. Look at them face to face, without fear, wishing for them to change.

    Simply facing what you find begins the alchemy of catharsis. ↗

  4. Light or nothing

    At rock bottom, don’t quit. Stay at the height of your soul.

    You are reborn in a way no one could have staged: trauma becomes θαῦμα, wonder. ↗

  5. In the labour

    Surrender, don’t fear, push, and have a good midwife: a teacher or a helper.

    Then the birth into light can happen at its ripe hour. ↗

  6. Keep on going

    When the path is hard, don’t stop.

    Athena’s advice to the younger Athena: you will find the way. ↗

Watch out for

  • Forcing it. Keep practising without scheduling the result: its hour cannot be programmed.
  • Getting lost in the dark. Athena calls depression a kind of deep rest, the soul’s cry to be freed, and in the same breath says to seek help so you don’t get lost there. That can mean a doctor or a therapist.

5

Φαναῖος

Phase 5: Glimpses

The veil opens for an instant, and closes again.

What it feels like

  • Moments of unity: in meditation, in nature, in love, sometimes in the middle of the washing-up.
  • Afterwards, ordinary life can feel flat, and you want the moment back.

What shifts

The glimpses stop being events and start being the ground you stand on.

What to do

  1. Rest after an opening

    Stay with it a while. Let yourself be nourished and strengthened before you act on it.

    Like a newborn’s first forty days, new consciousness needs strengthening. ↗

  2. Enjoy it without mistaking it

    Welcome visions, energy or an opening heart as steps, and don’t confuse them with awakening.

    Awakening is not an experience; it is a witness. ↗

  3. The apophatic test

    When you think “this is it”, pause and ask: am I still seeing or understanding something? If so, keep going: you have not reached Ithaca yet.

    The One is no “something”, so any object, even God or light, is not yet it. ↗

  4. Choose one direction

    Drop wanting to do everything. Keep one target, like the archer whose will has stopped changing.

    At the archer’s step the will stops wavering. ↗

  5. Steady the flashes

    Don’t chase the “wow” moments. Build a philosophical, virtuous and musical way of life around them, and hold it with a steady will.

    “It is raining awakenings”; what matters is a stable way of life. ↗

Watch out for

  • Collecting experiences. Energy, astral or out-of-body experiences are stages, not goals.
  • Saying “I am awakened”. Athena takes that sentence as a sign that the road continues.

εὐδαιμονία

Phase 6: Settling

Not learning something new: recognising what you already are.

What it feels like

  • Less drama. Things still happen and you still feel them, but they move the surface, not the depth.
  • Athena names three marks that do show: a universal, impersonal love; εὐθυμία, a childlike cheer that sees life as divine play; and indifference to goals that serve only the ego.

What shifts

There is nowhere further up to go. The only direction left is back.

What to do

  1. Know the knower

    Turn awareness back on itself. Where am I? What am I? Who thinks? Notice that you find no person or thing, only boundlessness, freedom, timelessness and peace. Repeat it.

    Athena calls it easy and doable now; repeated, it may open the exit of the cave. ↗

  2. Dis-identify

    Tell yourself: I have thoughts, emotions, desires and a body. Recall how much your body has changed while the “I” stayed. Rest as the one who is aware of them.

    You are not what you have. ↗

  3. Find your mast

    When something throws you up and down, notice how far, and come back to your inner axis.

    Eudaimonia is staying at your mast while fortune’s wheel turns. ↗

  4. Don’t stop at knowledge

    If you know the high ideas and the numbers, keep going until you unite with the source, and be ready to let the knowledge go.

    The Good is beyond knowledge; whoever stops to know it is stuck a stage before. ↗

Watch out for

  • Staying in the car. Knowledge is the vehicle that takes you to the foot of the mountain, and some people never get out and walk.
  • Mistaking calm for not caring. Ἀπάθεια is steadiness, not indifference: you simply stop investing your happiness in what others do or don’t do.

ἐμμένεια

Phase 7: The return

Back into the cave, to work, family and the tax office, and seeing the One there.

What it feels like

  • You are back in ordinary life, doing ordinary things, and they are no longer ordinary.
  • You can no longer work only for yourself.

What shifts

The circle closes, and opens again: “Every end of mine is my beginning.”

What to do

  1. Go back after the opening

    Return to work, loved ones and admin, and practise seeing the One and the fire there.

    Initiation is complete only with immanence. ↗

  2. Play the role, don’t become it

    Keep your duties, even strict or serious ones. Play them consciously, with the inner lightness of the eternal child.

    You play the role; you are not the role. ↗

  3. After every insight, work

    Ask which channel suits your soul (art, science, care, law, solidarity), and express the insight there.

    Awakening must be embodied, personally and politically. ↗

  4. Re-centre your goals

    Keep your aims, but take yourself out of the middle of them and put the whole there. Work for the benefit, freedom and awakening of all beings.

    Ego-goals serve someone who does not exist. ↗

  5. Live for the common good

    On waking, ask what you will do for the whole, not only your own circle. Gather with others in dialogue and philosophy.

    The last step is “not I, but we”. ↗

Watch out for

  • Staying above. Living in the experience of freedom and avoiding daily life; seeing the cave as “the others over there” is still dualism.
  • Using “all is one” as an excuse, either for nihilism (nothing matters) or for hedonism (anything goes). The ancients answered both with virtue.

A day on the path

Athena warns against picturing the awakened by their habits: no aura, diet, vocabulary or lifestyle marks them out, and anyone who calls themselves awakened is still on the road. So this is not a portrait of an enlightened person.

It is an ordinary weekday, with a job, a partner and a bad commute, lived with the practices on this page. The day is invented; every move in it is Athena’s.

Through the day

  • Name how your mind is working

    Whenever you remember: “now I am functioning this way, now that way”. Anxious, planning, comparing, enjoying.

    Bringing it from unconscious to conscious is walking towards the cave’s exit. ↗

  • Catch your conjectures

    When you read a mood into a frown or a silence, pause and ask whether the meaning came from them or from what you carry.

    Conjecture works from our own shadows and projections: the lowest level of the cave. ↗

  • “My dear One”

    At the tax office, in traffic, in a heated meeting: silently greet the One there, and tell yourself, “one more game”.

    Immanence means recognising the One in everything, including what annoys us. ↗

  • Watch your yeses and noes

    Notice each small yes and no as you give it.

    Their sum sets your course, as Heracles chose at the crossroads. ↗

  • Guard what goes in

    Choose the music, the sounds, the scents and the images you take in, instead of taking what comes. Prefer flowers, herbs and incense to synthetic perfume.

    Listening, Athena says, is the most important spiritual act: sound flows straight into the soul and shapes character. ↗

  • Ask a craving what it wants

    When a desire pulls, ask what fullness it is really after (rest, company, recognition), and look for that fullness within.

    Behind every desire the soul is seeking the Good. ↗

  • Love in four registers

    A good word to everyone, a good thought about everyone, a feeling of love towards everyone, and help in action wherever you can.

    This is the natural ethics of someone who sees consciousness as common. ↗

  • Let go of being right

    When you catch yourself wanting to be proved right, remember you have probably lost justice already, and aim for balance instead.

    “Where I want to find my rights, I have probably lost justice.” ↗

  • Die a little

    Once a day, pick one identification (a role, a label, a belief, an image of your body), let it go for a moment, and ask: what am I beyond what I seem to be?

    Philosophy is a practice of dying, and Socrates means daily. ↗

  • An evening alone

    A few hours without phone or television. Let what you have pushed down come up, and listen to your conscience.

    It isn’t loneliness we flee but conscience: alone, without distraction, it starts to speak. ↗

  • A real conversation

    Meet in person with people who share the wish. Read together, and ask together.

    “We are a lesche”: a place where people talk philosophy for the common good. ↗

  • Poetry next to philosophy

    Read a poem alongside the week’s philosophy.

    Analysis alone will not do it; poetry comes closest to the ineffable. ↗

  • Open one Greek word

    Take one word (ἐλευθερία, εὐδαιμονία, ἔρως), unpack its parts and roots, and draw out the teaching folded into it.

    Following Proclus, the meaning can be reached through the word itself. ↗

  • Beauty on purpose

    Walk among old stones or by the sea, listen to harmonious music, read the ancients. Take none of it for granted.

    Eros is woken by the beautiful. ↗

  • One act at your own scale

    Something for your street, your village or your community: show up where people reason together.

    Abstaining from politics is itself a political stance. ↗

The meditations, step by step

Five of Athena’s practices for the morning sit, from the simplest. The minutes are suggestions.

  • Eyes open, eyes closed

    about 5 minutes · to begin with

    1. Open your eyes and note what is there: “something, something, many somethings”.
    2. Close them, and ask: is there “something” here, or a boundless expanse?
    3. Expect darkness at first. With training, Athena says, it becomes luminous. ↗

  • Ὄν, a chant to start

    2 minutes · before any of the others

    1. Chant “on”, slowly.
    2. Feel the N vibrate on the palate.
    3. Rest in the vibration when the sound stops. ↗

  • Know the knower

    10 minutes

    1. Turn your awareness back on itself.
    2. Ask, slowly: Where am I? What am I? What am I made of? Who thinks?
    3. Try to become aware of the awareness you are.
    4. Notice that you find no person and no thing: only boundlessness, freedom, timelessness and peace.
    5. Stay there. Repeat it daily. ↗

  • Into emptiness

    10 to 15 minutes

    1. Sit, and don’t fill the meditation with pictures (a beach, a light, the kitchen).
    2. Each time content comes, let it go, and move towards purity of mind.
    3. Rest as mind resting in itself, without content. ↗

  • The ether meditation

    20 minutes · the fullest

    1. Eyes open: look at the objects in the room and how they stand to you.
    2. Shift your attention from the objects to the space they sit in. Notice the body relax, the heart open, the gaze widen.
    3. Close your eyes, find the breath, and feel the space inside.
    4. Place one short thought or image at the centre of the mind’s screen, and breathe with it.
    5. Shift attention from that thought to the space it appears in, which is there before it and after it.
    6. Recognise its qualities: vast, open, softly alive, luminous, timeless. Recognise it as the “I” that sees and hears.
    7. Feel that space through the neck, the heart, the hands, the belly, the back, the legs, the soles: the space breathes the body.
    8. Let body, awareness and space merge into the present: the inner and the outer space are one.
    9. Open your eyes, and keep the recognition as the forms start to move again. ↗

When life hits

Athena’s teaching is at its most practical in the hard moments. This is what it says to do in some of them.

None of this replaces medical or psychological care. Athena, speaking of depression, says to seek help so you don’t get lost there.

  • When you lose someone or something

    1. Pause before you fix anything.
    2. Ask what condition the pain is calling you to change.
    3. Let it push you out of the state you were in. Athena compares psychic pain to training: it hurts, and it leads to no pain. ↗

    “Your sun was never in danger.” ↗

  • When the floor gives way

    1. Treat the shock as ἐξαίφνης, the sudden event, and ask what it is waking in you.
    2. Remember that every situation has an exit of freedom.
    3. See the crisis as a cut between past and future, and watch for the new ideas hatching in it.
    4. If outer things close down overnight, ask where the basis of your well-being really is. ↗

    “Transformations always follow a crisis.” ↗

  • When you are low for a long time

    1. Consider it as the soul’s cry to be freed: a mask that has stuck, asking to come off.
    2. Allow it to be deep rest, rather than a failure.
    3. Seek help, so you don’t get lost there: a friend, a teacher, a doctor, a therapist. ↗

  • When someone wrongs you

    1. Don’t become indignant, and don’t fight them.
    2. Make room for it. Light it with understanding: why did it happen, why did they do it? Put yourself in their place.
    3. Embrace it with love and stop fighting it, because as long as we fight something we hold it close.
    4. Then decide with discernment what to do, even if that means leaving. ↗

  • When someone comes at you

    1. Don’t shout and don’t hit. Let your bearing carry the force.
    2. Keep wisdom at your centre, and set clear boundaries at your edges.
    3. Name the people who protect you: they are your ἕρκος, your fence. The greater the treasure, the higher the walls. ↗

    “Our centre is wisdom, but our boundaries must be red.” ↗

  • When you have got it wrong

    1. Say: I’m human, I erred, I deceived myself here.
    2. Ask what you can learn, adjust, and move on.
    3. Be glad you feel the shame at all: it is justice regulating itself in you. Feeling nothing is the real danger. ↗

  • When something shakes you, or sweeps you up

    1. Notice it.
    2. Recall that only the soul’s motions are moved, never its essence: calm is uncovered, not built.
    3. Try the ἔστω, “let it be”: as a working hypothesis, assume this is perfect and just, that you are partway up the mountain and can’t see all its folds, and that it may bring good things. Say “let it be”, and let go. ↗

  • When you are fed up with everything

    1. Read the exhaustion as a signal: “this can’t be all” is the push to look for what is true.
    2. Stop looking for the exit in your circumstances, and turn the attention inward. ↗

    “You are the gate that leads to your freedom.” ↗

  • When you disagree

    1. Before you discuss, define: what do each of you mean by the word you are fighting over?
    2. Don’t avoid the conflict. Look for the union of the opposites in front of you. ↗

    “Disagreement is the beginning of agreement.” ↗

  • When you are afraid of dying

    1. Prepare in peacetime: cultivate courage and clarity now, the way you clear snow you know will fall.
    2. Face each cowardice, anger and rage while you are alive, with meditation, hymns and inner work, so the passage holds nothing to fear. ↗

    “There is no death: only sleep, dream and awakening.” ↗

Love, and the people close to you

For Athena, eros is the engine of the whole path, and the quality of your love shows in a set order: first in how you treat yourself, then your partner, then your colleagues and everyone else, then society. ↗

  • Start with yourself

    Treat yourself with sacredness, and train daily in love and kindness towards yourself. Then notice how it changes the respect you have for the people you love, and theirs for you.

    As we treat our partners within, so we treat our partners without. ↗

  • Be the ally of their freedom

    When you love someone, ask: am I the ally of their freedom? When you hear yourself think “you will do what I want”, call it what it is, control. Then help their freedom actively, as far as you can.

    To love means: I am the ally of your freedom. ↗

  • Define before you discuss

    Before talking about love, ask what it is. Notice whether you mean attachment, jealousy or criticism, and go back to the word’s roots.

    Otherwise one word covers opposite states. ↗

  • Know eros instead of avoiding it

    Get to know desire truly and from within, under a teaching that gives you tools and criteria. Don’t make it the one part of you left out of the path.

    Then eros lifts you instead of burning you. ↗

  • Count everyone’s well-being

    Notice whose well-being you count, and treat other people’s as equal to your own.

    If we are one being, selective openness makes no sense. ↗

  • Let love widen

    Love a partner and a family; then the values, institutions and laws of your city; then the universe.

    Diotima’s ladder: eros grows from the private to the political to the cosmic. ↗

  • Meet everyone with reverence

    Approach every being with reverence, and with a gaze that remembers that Being is common to you both.

    It changes how your own inner energy moves. ↗

Work, and the world

Athena is clear that awakening has to be lived, personally and politically. This is what that means from nine to five, and beyond. ↗

  • Find your own work

    Come to your centre and ask what you love to do. Two signs: do I burn with zeal for it, and doing it, do I lose the sense of time? Follow that, leaving money aside for the question.

    Every soul is born to fulfil its own work. ↗

  • Turn βίος into ζωή

    Ask what you love to do, what inspires you, and how you can benefit society, and follow your deep heartbeat rather than routine.

    Life becomes conscious when it follows love and understanding rather than instinct and habit. ↗

  • Govern yourself first

    Practise self-mastery before you try to run anything outside you: a team, a family, a project.

    The first polity we must learn to govern is our soul. ↗

  • Play the role, don’t become it

    Be the manager, the employee, the expert, fully, and remember who is playing.

    We play a unique role and make an ethos, but we are the one playing, not the role. ↗

  • Speak simply

    Prefer plain words to academic ones, in meetings as in philosophy.

    Simplicity is the easiest road to truth. ↗

  • Take political responsibility

    Notice when you say “I’m not political”. Remember that politics includes how you judge and think, and act at your own scale: your town, your village, your community.

    Abstaining is itself a political stance. ↗

  • Don’t shrink into your small circle

    Feel responsible for the whole earth, as Kazantzakis did.

    The ancient ἰδιώτης, the idiot, is the one who forgets he is one with all. ↗

  • If you teach or raise children

    Light your own light first, and shine it on them rather than filling them with information.

    As the sun wakes sight in the eye, a teacher wakes what is already known within. ↗
